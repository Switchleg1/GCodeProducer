# Walls and hole boundaries

**Where:** Process → Walls. **Stage:** `fdm_perimeters` (core).
Walls are generated by insetting the filled slice region, including all its holes, before solid and sparse infill. The deepest requested inset becomes the working fill boundary.

## Settings

| Setting | Default | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Wall loops (`perimeters`) | 2 | Requested contour loop count; 1–20 |
| Wall line width (`line_width`) | `auto` | Nozzle/job width, or an explicit 0.05–3 mm width |
| Variable-width walls (`variable_width`) | On | Thin folded loops and print tapering centre beads in narrow features; see below |
| Minimum bead width (`min_bead_width`) | 50% | Narrowest variable bead, % of line width (25–100%); thinner tips stay empty |
| Maximum bead width (`max_bead_width`) | 150% | Widest gap-fill centre bead, % of line width (100–200%) |

The nozzle profile normally supplies 0.45 mm width. Job width falls back to
1.1 × nozzle diameter when the nozzle's numeric width is nonpositive. The
process setting's `auto` means use that job width. Narrow features are handled
separately by variable-width walls (below).

## How contours are offset

A wall centreline must lie inside material, half a bead width away from its
boundary. Outer contours therefore move **inward**, while contours around
holes move **outward into the surrounding part**. Moving a hole wall inward
into the empty hole would shrink the opening and create unsupported paths;
that is not the intended behaviour.

Inner loops print before outer loops on each layer. Paths
are tagged `inner_perimeter` or `outer_perimeter`; the Output stage assigns
their requested speeds independently. Changing support reach cannot correct
a wall placed on the wrong side of a hole, so inspect hole geometry directly.

Two 0.45 mm walls have approximately 0.9 mm nominal combined bead width, but
curvature, offsets and thin-feature clipping affect the exact result. This
is not a dimensional-tolerance guarantee.

## Variable-width walls

With fixed-width offsets a narrowing feature (fan blade tip, lettering, the
top of a stepped wall) has a loop that folds back on itself where the
material is narrower than two line widths, so its sides overlap and
over-extrude, and no loop at all where it is narrower than one line width, so
the tip is empty. **Variable-width walls** keep exactly the same loops (same
paths, same order) and only vary the flow:

- **line width ≤ D < 2 × line width** (`D` = material width across the loop):
  the folded loop's sides print at `D / 2`, no less than the minimum bead
  width, so they no longer overlap.
- **D < line width**, where no loop reaches: an extra centre bead follows the
  middle of the tip, its width following `D` down to the minimum bead width.

Lettering and other strokes therefore keep their original continuous
outlines. The maximum bead width applies to gap fill (Process → Infill),
which has no original lines to keep and may use one centre bead up to that
width. Narrow zones are found as the material minus its opening at one line
width, the centre line as the medial axis of the boundary. Flow follows the
width segment by segment; feeds are capped for the widest bead in the path.
The Preview draws every bead at the nominal width. Turn the option off to get
the plain fixed-width loops.

## Thin features and interaction with fill

Requested loops may not all fit around a narrow rim or between a hole and the
outside boundary. Insets can split at narrow webs or disappear where there is
not enough room. Variable-width walls (above) and **Infill → Gap fill** fill
what remains narrow. Adding more loops may remove all remaining infill area;
increasing overlap does not recover missing wall geometry.

After changing wall count or width, inspect:

- outside dimensional shape and hole sizes;
- rims around internal openings;
- narrow webs that may disappear;
- the interface between innermost walls and solid/sparse fill;
- volumetric caps when wider lines are requested.

If the model contains open contours, duplicate faces or intersecting geometry,
repair the STL before relying on offsets. Wall offsets cannot repair an incorrect slice outline.
